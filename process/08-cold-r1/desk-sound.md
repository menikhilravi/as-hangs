# Cold round 1 · desktop 1440×900 with sound (18 runs, 7 with audio, published halfway build) — condensed

First 10 s: fade from near black to sunset; "a lonely arch on a salt flat with its upside-down twin under a bare bulb. Quiet and
dreamlike." The weight drops at 2 s, a turret floats in at 3–4 s, the quote at 5–6 s: "I understood the idea in 10 seconds with no
instructions. A genuine 'oh!'" Felt calm and a bit awed; "the picture is beautiful". Wanted to grab the chain or yank the ball.

Understood: pull a link down to hang a weight there (the pull sets the weight); the arch mirrors the chain live; each weight becomes a
turret; heavy weights or yanks collapse it and it is rebuilt on a scaffold in the new shape; weights can never be removed.

Delighted: the fade-up and the picture; the silent demo plus quote; "the arch bending as I pulled: I could feel the load passing between
the two halves"; the first crash ("guilty oops, then relief"); new shapes ("designing a cathedral by hanging potatoes on a chain");
"gentle mode: turrets seating themselves without anything breaking".

Concrete problems (weight these):
- Clicking does nothing; you have to drag. Some drags right next to the chain registered nothing (narrow grab target).
- **Every normal pull (≥30 px) destroyed the arch; it took ~15 tries to learn tiny pulls are safe — "a few pixels of luck".
  Their ONE change: show how heavy the weight is while you drag, and let the arch visibly strain or crack once past the point where it
  will fall, so turret vs collapse becomes a deliberate choice.**
- Grabbing the existing ball doesn't move or remove it; no way to take weights off or start over.
- 5–10 s of silent rubble after every collapse; the whole crash-and-rebuild takes 20–35 s and is identical by the 4th time.
- Still picture when idle (2 min, nothing moves).
- Turrets look stuck on (drums + cone on a slanted wedge, at an angle to the outside of the arch); some float with a gap, others clip.
  Many weights make a messy overlapping clump. Spires run off the top of the screen.
- Turret pieces hang in the sky far from the arch for a while ("floating junk"). The scaffold pops in with posts through the rubble.
- "Under load the arch bends like a bead necklace before it breaks, so it never feels like stone."
- Grain and ragged edges everywhere: fuzzy sawtooth block outlines, speckled links, stair-stepped bulb, jagged ground band
  (src has speckle/edge fixes; verify). Stones look like sugar cubes or marshmallows.
- A thin dark squiggle on the cellar wall right of the bulb ("a hair on the screen").
- Backdrop flat: mesas are one-colour cutouts; the salt flat looks like static; the arch's shadows run to the horizon as two blue
  stripes "like ski tracks".
- The crash hits maximum volume (slightly above once); otherwise only a faint hiss.
- Harness measured 22–25 fps during the opening and collapses (61 idle) — testers ran in parallel; re-measure.
- The bulb looks like a pull-cord light but does nothing. The quote shows once and disappears for good.

Would send: yes, to architecture/engineering/calm-toy friends: "Hang a weight on the chain and watch the arch. Gently first, then not
gently. Robert Hooke worked this out in 1675."
