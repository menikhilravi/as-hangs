# 01 · What the two earlier attempts teach (≈10 minutes with each)

## Cosmogenesis (../random) — "ok"
- 22 chapters, each a remake of a demo a knowledgeable person has seen (Gargantua black hole, Gray–Scott leopard spots,
  Lenia, boids). The thumbnails are handsome and interchangeable.
- About 40% of every screen is chrome: a HUD, Story/Science tabs, sliders. The page tells you what to find wonderful.
- You adjust parameters; you never *do* anything that means something.

## Ovation (../great) — "very average at best"
- Final screenshots (`.snap/v6/c-standing.png`): 1,700 SDF figures with identical mannequin faces, flat ivory tiers,
  a debug HUD. It reads as a cheap game crowd, which is exactly the risk the brief named: emotion that depends on
  believable humans can't be carried by procedural geometry.
- The build log is 90% mechanics (Kuramoto clapping, economies of love, 56 formant shouts, a budget, encores) and 10% look.
  The cold-tester notes kept reporting "mannequins", "black void", "particle effect", and the work answered with more systems.
- The verb (tap = bow) was found by accident by most testers and carries little meaning on its own.
- Agent testers reported delight from screenshots and spectrograms. Their concrete complaints were right; their praise wasn't evidence.

## Rules I'm taking into stage 1
1. **Fidelity first.** Pick a subject whose payoff is light, matter, sound, space or type: things code computes *exactly*,
   not things it imitates. Prefer subjects whose real-world look is itself soft or abstract (thermal imagery,
   projected light, stone in sun, glowing metal), because there a correct model *is* a photograph.
2. **One verb that means something.** If the verb could be swapped for a button without loss, the idea is dead.
3. **No chrome.** If it needs a panel, a slider or a legend, it isn't finished.
4. **Look before systems.** Spikes prove the image and the sound, nothing else.
5. **Distrust praise.** Weight concrete complaints from testers; ignore their adjectives.
