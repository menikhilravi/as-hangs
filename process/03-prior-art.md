# 03 · Prior art (web searches by four research agents)

The session's WebSearch budget (200 calls, shared by all agents) ran out during this round. Where a concept got no searches,
its entry says so; those verdicts are provisional and lean on pages fetched directly plus memory. No search engine was
queried through WebFetch to get around the limit.

Verdict key: **DONE** (a knowledgeable person has seen it) · **PARTLY DONE** (some part exists) · **NOT FOUND** (honest searching found nothing close) · **UNVERIFIED** (no searches possible).

## Concepts 1–8

Searched fully: 1–3. Concept 4 got 3 searches. 5–8 got none (budget exhausted); their entries rest on fetched known pages.

### 1 · AS HANGS — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **Gaudí's polyfunicular hanging model over a mirror** (Colònia Güell model, reconstructed 1983; another over a mirror in
  La Pedrera's attic). The hang-below / stand-above mirror is Gaudí's own museum display, static.
  <https://lapedreracasamila.com/casa-mila-espai-gaudi-attic-museum/>
- **CADenary**, Axel Kilian (MIT), 2002–04: a Java applet simulating hanging strings with editable weights, explicitly a digital
  Gaudí model. A CAD tool: no mirror, no stone, no interior, no sound. <https://www.axelkilian.com/projectpages/cadenary.html>
- **Gaudí, the Atelier of the Divine**, GEDEON Experiences, 2023: location-based VR with ropes hanging from a ceiling and a floor
  mirror showing them as architecture. You watch; you don't build. <https://blog.vive.com/us/gaudi-the-atelier-of-the-divine-vr-syncs-with-lothlorien/>
- **"Hang it, flip it, build it"**, an unbuilt room idea in eyal-weiss/wonderlattice (issue #61, opened 2026-09-28): hang a beaded
  chain from pegs, add weights, flip it into a stone arch. The 2D core of this concept, proposed publicly two days ago.
  <https://github.com/eyal-weiss/wonderlattice/issues/61>
- Professional tools: Kangaroo (Piker), RhinoVAULT, eQUILIBRIUM (Block Research Group). Weaker: archi-civil "Gaudí Funicular Arch
  Builder" (2D slider); CLERESTORY (CSAF, itch.io, Sept 2026), a cathedral builder with thrust and buttresses but no chains.
- Still new: pulling a chain raises the stone building live across the mirror; chains hung from chains become vaults; you walk in
  and hear reverb computed from the form.

### 2 · OBSCURA — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **Cameras and Lenses**, Bartosz Ciechanowski, Dec 2020: interactive pinhole demos (inversion, bigger hole brighter and
  blurrier, a lens makes it sharp) in an abstract box. Every physics beat of this concept, minus the room and multiple holes.
  <https://ciechanow.ski/cameras-and-lenses/>
- **Camera Obscura series**, Abelardo Morell, 1991–: exactly the image, as photographs.
  <https://www.lensculture.com/articles/abelardo-morell-outside-in-upside-down-and-now-in-color>
- Classroom pinhole simulators (physandbox, Molander applet); walk-in camera obscuras; "An Obscure Camera" (viewers open portholes onto
  a live projection). Eclipse crescents under trees: much photographed (ESA, 2026), no interactive found.
- Still new: a first-person dark room where you pierce your own holes, overlapping live inverted worlds over furniture, a crescent per
  hole in an eclipse, a Niépce plate at the end.

### 3 · STILL WARM — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **Home Detective** (Quest mixed reality, 2023): a "Residual Heat Scanner" shows criminals' recent movements over your own
  living room. Same verb (read leftover heat to learn what happened), as a procedural crime game. <https://adventuregamehotspot.com/game/694/home-detective>
- **Thermal Gestures**, Marco Pinter, 2016–: touch a fabric wall and your heat residue is projected back, fading as it cools. Exactly
  "my touches leave warm prints that fade". <https://www.marcopinter.com/Thermal.html>
- Cyberpunk 2077 braindance thermal layer (2020); research reading the past from residual heat: "What Happened 3 Seconds Ago?"
  (CVPR 2023) <https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13651>, departure time from heat traces (Sensors 2020); Richard Mosse, *Incoming* (aesthetic).
- Still new: a wordless story told only in heat that decays on a real Newton-cooling clock while you read it.

### 4 · ARBORGLYPH — PARTLY DONE (3 searches)
- Closest: **Giuseppe Penone, *It Will Continue to Grow Except at That Point***, 1968: a steel cast of his hand in a tree trunk that the
  tree grows around. The canonical work on a human mark reshaped by growth. <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Penone>
- Shallow virtual carving: Forest of Love (shared carving forest), Treemail (iPhone, 2011), Canal du Midi donors' virtual tree (2021).
- Still new: a century of growth widening, scarring and burying letters that stay at the same height.

### 5 · HOLD STILL — UNVERIFIED (no searches)
- Daguerre, *Boulevard du Temple* (1838); Michael Wesely's years-long open-shutter photographs (1990s–2004); Superhot (2016, the inverse
  rule); Sally Mann's memoir *Hold Still* (2015), a title clash.

### 6 · ETCH — UNVERIFIED (no searches)
- Real etching only (nitric acid / ferric chloride). **Fact correction:** cooling rates are roughly 100–10,000 °C per million years
  (Wikipedia), not "about a degree"; the structure can't be made in a lab. Concept text corrected.

### 7 · SLOW LIGHT — PARTLY DONE (provisional, no searches)
- Closest: **Femto-photography**, Raskar, Velten et al., MIT Media Lab, 2011: the reference footage, as video.
  <https://web.media.mit.edu/~raskar/trillionfps/>
- Abramson's light-in-flight holography (1970s); **mitransient** (Royo et al., 2022–25), an offline Mitsuba renderer for light in flight
  <https://github.com/diegoroyo/mitransient>; Jarabo et al. 2014. Unchecked: a real-time browser/Shadertoy version.

### 8 · THUNDER — UNVERIFIED (no searches)
- Ribner & Roy, "Acoustics of thunder: a quasilinear model for tortuous lightning" (JASA, 1982): essentially this synthesis method, in
  the literature. Few's "string of pearls" model; Farnell's procedural thunder (*Designing Sound*, 2010). No web toy checked.

## Concepts 9–16

Searched: 9–12 (6–19 searches each). 13–16 got none (budget exhausted); their links were fetched directly.

### 9 · THIN ICE — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **"Rock thrown onto frozen lake makes coolest sound ever"**, Cory Williams, 2014 (the viral "pew" video, ~11M views).
  <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D3Ex5bcmiEc>
- Also: Henrik Trygg / Mårten Ajne's black-ice skating film (2018) <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oUTrA8M6f40>; NPR Skunk Bear
  "Why Does A Frozen Lake Sound Like A Star Wars Blaster?" (2016; the lake "singing on its own") <https://www.npr.org/2016/12/21/506305383/why-does-a-frozen-lake-sound-like-a-star-wars-blaster>;
  Ben Burtt's blaster (1977, a struck guy wire); a 2023 paper modelling the flexural chirp for ice-thickness estimation.
- 19 searches found no interactive that synthesises singing-ice chirps physically. A playable lake would be new.

### 10 · GATHER — DONE (as a mechanic)
- Closest: **Glass Blown: Arcade Challenge**, Hedrosgames (HTML5): hold to blow, arrows to rotate, shape before it cools.
  <https://hedrosgames.itch.io/glass-blown>
- Also: A Tale in the Desert's glassblowing (c. 2008: rotate or it sags, read heat by colour); "Glassblower's Breath" (three.js + mic);
  Chihuly app (2013, blow into the mic); Pittsburgh Glass Center + CMU VR (2023); mobile Glassblower! (2020). Only fidelity is left.

### 11 · TOTALITY — PARTLY DONE (mostly)
- Closest: **Dan McGlaun's Eclipse Simulator** (eclipse2024.org, c. 2020–24): full-sky views, sky darkening, Baily's beads from LRO limb
  data, diamond ring, coronas, time sliders. <https://www.astronomy.com/observing/simulate-what-totality-will-look-like-during-the-2024-solar-eclipse/>
- Also: a websim "drag the Moon" eclipse toy; Lunar Delight's simulator (2026); Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 (umbra from altitude);
  360° VR of the real shadow arriving (High Desert Eclipse, 2017).
- Still new: only a first-person landscape where your drag sends the umbra racing toward you.

### 12 · FIRST LIGHT — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **Stargazer Simulator**, TeamCosmicDawn (Steam, planned 2027): first-person telescope game with seeing affecting detail;
  unreleased. <https://store.steampowered.com/app/4455610/_/>
- Also: Aberrator (Cor Berrevoets; diffraction and aberrations, no seeing); Damian Peach's static Saturn seeing simulations (2003–04);
  Stelvision Telescope Simulator (static, explicitly no turbulence or averted vision); Stellarium oculars.
- Not found: averted vision as a mechanic; live seeing + diffraction + focus in one faithful web eyepiece.

### 13 · LIGHTS OUT — PARTLY DONE (low confidence, no searches)
- Closest: **Darkened Cities (Villes éteintes)**, Thierry Cohen, 2010–19: night photos of cities composited with the stars seen at their
  latitude from the desert. Exactly the end image, as photographs. <https://www.thierrycohen.com/pages/work/starlights.html>
- Also: Academo's interactive Bortle-scale slider <http://academo.org/demos/bortle-scale/>; Globe at Night magnitude charts; SKYGLOW project.
- Not found (unsearched): switching off a city block by block with skyglow computed from what's left.

### 14 · EVERY SUNSET — PARTLY DONE (low confidence, no searches)
- Closest: **Lunar Eclipse 3D Simulator**, Marco Bastoni, 2026: a viewpoint on the Moon showing Earth eclipsing the Sun, with refraction,
  Rayleigh and ozone modelled. <https://acacciadieclissi.space/simulators/lunar-eclipse-3d/index.html>
- Real photographs: Surveyor 3 (1967), Apollo 12 (1969), Blue Ghost Mission 1 (2025, "the ring of light around the Earth").
- Not found: mapping the ring to real places and zooming to where it's evening now.

### 15 · 52 HZ — theme DONE; interactive NOT FOUND (low confidence, no searches)
- Closest: **52 Hertz**, Malisa (itch.io, 2016), a narrative game about being unheard, no propagation mechanic
  <https://malisa-nash.itch.io/52-hertz>; **Ecco the Dolphin** (Sega, 1992), the canonical "sing into the ocean" mechanic.
- Also: *The Loneliest Whale* (2021); BTS "Whalien 52" (2015); *Whale 52* (Plympton, 2025); Google Pattern Radio.
- Not found: a call ray-traced through a modelled SOFAR channel where the wrong frequency is the mechanic.

### 16 · NOON — PARTLY DONE (low confidence, no searches)
- Closest: **Llorenç Barber's city concerts** (música plurifocal, from the late 1980s): pieces for a whole city's bell towers, heard from the
  streets. <https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloren%C3%A7_Barber>
- Also: Janet Cardiff, *The Forty Part Motet* (2001: walking changes the mix); Bill Fontana's distance-based sound sculptures; Charles Ives
  hearing bands from opposite sides of Danbury square.
- Not found: a draggable listener with speed-of-sound delays from real bell positions.

## Concepts 17–24

### 17 · STALACPIPE — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **"Hidden Melodies"**, Global Sound Movement (UCLan), 2019. Recorded struck stalactites in St Michael's Cave, Gibraltar;
  an exhibition soundboard let visitors play them. <https://www.chronicle.gi/global-sound-movement-recreate-sounds-stalactites-st-michaels-cave/>
- Also: Morrowind: Bloodmoon "Cave of Hidden Music" (Bethesda, 2003): strike stalagmites to repeat a melody
  <https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Bloodmoon:The_Ritual_of_Earth>. "Stalactites", Andrés Padilla Domene, 2017 (ambisonic installation)
  <https://andrespadilla.net/en/stalactites-eng/>. "Chime", lazyshpee (itch.io cave soundtoy) <https://lazyshpee.itch.io/chime>.
- Still new: a lithophone where each note is framed by its growth time. Thin.

### 18 · ALEPH — DONE
- Closest: **"Ox House Water"**, cjros / chrisjz, August 2026. You type your name, drag a timeline back ~4,000 years, and each
  letter morphs through Roman, archaic Greek, Phoenician and Proto-Sinaitic forms into its Egyptian picture.
  <https://cjros.itch.io/ox-house-water> · <https://chrisjz.github.io/ox-house-water/>
- Also: UsefulCharts "Evolution of the Alphabet" poster (2019); Funtranslator Proto-Sinaitic converter; "Ox, House, Stick" (Don Robb, 2007).
- Made six weeks ago, almost exactly as written. Dead.

### 19 · STRATA — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **OED Text Visualizer**, Oxford University Press, 2020 (beta). Paste up to 500 words; each word is placed on a timeline
  by first recorded use and coloured by origin. Same data mapping, drawn as a scatter chart. <https://oed-text-visualizer.oxfordlanguages.com/>
- Also: Merriam-Webster Time Traveler (2017); Jonathan Reeve's Macro-Etymological Analyzer (2013)
  <https://jonreeve.com/2013/11/introducing-the-macro-etymological-analyzer/>; Ideas Illustrated "Visualizing English Word Origins" (2012).
- Still new: only the geological-section presentation. The idea is done.

### 20 · MOBILE — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **Hanna Sandin**, parse-tree Calder mobiles, e.g. "Shared-forest for an incomplete sentence" (2008), physical sculpture.
  <https://www.airgallery.org/past-fellows/hanna-sandin>
- Also: dirkenglund/physics-mobile (torque-balanced web mobile, no text) <https://github.com/dirkenglund/physics-mobile>;
  Math Cats Virtual Calder Mobile; Ken Perlin's Kinetic Poetry <https://cs.nyu.edu/~perlin/experiments/poetry/>.
- Still new: a typed sentence parsed into a live balanced mobile.

### 21 · PIVOT — PARTLY DONE (provisional: no searches)
- Closest: **The Moving Sofa Problem page**, Dan Romik (UC Davis), updated 2026: animations of every candidate sofa, a notebook,
  and printable STLs. <https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~romik/movingsofa/> · Baek's proof <https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19826>
- Also: Friends "PIVOT!" (1999); Moving Out (Team17, 2020); Quanta, Feb 2025.
- Unchecked: browser demos where you push or design a sofa (GeoGebra/Desmos/itch).

### 22 · KETTLE — UNVERIFIED (no searches)
- Context only, not games: Gridwatch <https://gridwatch.co.uk/>; Kate Morley's National Grid: Live <https://grid.iamkate.com/>;
  Wikipedia "TV pickup" confirms the 2,800 MW surge after the 1990 England v West Germany shootout; Dinorwig 0 → 1,800 MW in ~16 s.

### 23 · UNTETHERED — PARTLY DONE (provisional: no searches)
- Closest: **Kerbal Space Program** (Squad, 2011/2015): EVA manoeuvring under orbital physics, buried in a big sim.
- Also: Gemini 4 (1965): McDivitt thrust toward the spent stage and drifted away, the real story behind the idea; *Gravity* (2013);
  SpaceX ISS Docking Simulator (browser, no orbital-relative motion) <https://iss-sim.spacex.com/>.

### 24 · CRANK — PARTLY DONE (provisional: no searches)
- Closest: **Bang Goes the Theory, "Human Power Station"**, BBC, 2009.
- Also: Buckminster Fuller's "energy slaves" (1940); Low-tech Magazine's solar-powered website (2018)
  <https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/>; Spaceplan (Jake Hollands, 2016), a clicker that escalates power sources.

## Concepts 25–31

Searched: 25–27 (10–23 searches each). 28–31 got none (budget exhausted); every link for them was fetched directly.

### 25 · LINOTYPE — NOT FOUND
- Nearest: **LetterMpress**, John Bonadies, 2011 (iPad/Mac virtual letterpress with hand-set wood type; no line-casting)
  <https://makezine.com/article/craft/lettermpress_a_virtual_letterp/>; RIT's *19th-century Printing Press Experience VR* (Steam, 2025,
  an Albion hand press); *Linotype: The Film* (Doug Wilson, 2012); Hanx Writer (sampled typewriter, 2014); a static Linotype scan on Sketchfab.
- 23 searches found no interactive line-casting machine anywhere (web, itch.io, Steam, GitHub, VR). Assembling matrices from typed text,
  spaceband justification, the cast slug, and physically modelled clatter would all be new.

### 26 · SCRAPE — PARTLY DONE
- Closest: **the wallpaper "dig" at 97 Orchard Street**, Tenement Museum, 1994: 22–23 wallpaper layers and up to 39 paint layers,
  used to tell the tenants' stories. <https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2008/mayjune/curio/wallflower>
- Also: Graffiti Archaeology (Cassidy Curtis/Stamen, ~2003, interactive wall stratigraphy by photos); Gordon Matta-Clark, *Walls Paper*
  (1972); Richard McGuire, *Here* (1989/2014, one corner of a room across millennia); *Unpacking* (Witch Beam, 2021); "5 Layers" (itch.io).
- Still new: hands-on scraping through a home's dated layers to reveal its households.

### 27 · NOT A PLACE OF HONOR — DONE
- Closest: **This Is Not A Place Of Honour**, Connor Sherlock, 2020: a first-person walk through the warning landscape quoting Sandia.
  <https://connor-sherlock.itch.io/this-is-not-a-place-of-honour>
- Plus at least four more itch.io games and RPGs on the same theme (Santi_T, lrenaissanceman et al., Mortdelnord 2025, Sea Change Games),
  *Into Eternity* (2010), 99% Invisible "Ten Thousand Years" (2014). A worn game-jam theme. Dead.

### 28 · MURPHY — PARTLY DONE (provisional, no searches)
- Closest: **MythBusters** (2005): toast pushed off a table edge tends to flip once and land butter-down. Not interactive.
- Matthews, "Tumbling toast, Murphy's Law and the fundamental constants" (Eur. J. Phys., 1995; Ig Nobel 1996)
  <https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0143-0807/16/4/005>; his 2001 schools experiment (≥62% butter-down); *I Am Bread* (2015).
- No interactive tumbling-toast sim found on the pages checked (including itch.io's "toast" tag). Unconfirmed.

### 29 · DUNK — PARTLY DONE (provisional, no searches)
- Closest: **The Perfect Dunk**, 17Shinsen, 2023 (Playdate "biscuit dunking simulator": crank it in, too long and it breaks)
  <https://17shinsen.itch.io/the-perfect-dunk>; **Dunk the Biscuit** (Brackeys jam, 2025) <https://anthonycode.itch.io/dunk-the-biscuit>;
  Just Dunk It! (2025). Len Fisher's research (Nature, 1999; Ig Nobel).
- The mechanic is a jam staple; a physics-driven version wasn't found.

### 30 · BRONZE — PARTLY DONE (provisional, no searches)
- Closest: **Do Not Touch**, Studio Moniker, 2013: every visitor's cursor is recorded and replayed among millions of earlier ones.
  <https://studiomoniker.com/projects/do-not-touch>
- Also: "Edit Wear and Read Wear" (Hill et al., CHI 1992), the founding idea of digital patina; Lozano-Hemmer, *Pulse Index* (2010);
  One Million Checkboxes (2024), r/place, The Button; the real Molly Malone statue, rubbed shiny for luck.
- A single bronze polished by every visitor's touches wasn't found.

### 31 · FREEFALL — PARTLY DONE (provisional, no searches)
- Closest: **"Free Fall"**, Guillaume Néry with Julie Gautier, 2010: the viral film of the freefall into Dean's Blue Hole.
- Also: **DEEP** (breath-controlled VR, 2016–) <https://www.exploredeep.com/>; neal.fun *The Deep Sea* (2019); *The Deepest Breath* (2023);
  *The Big Blue* (1988). Commercial spearfishing games probably have breath-hold mechanics (unverified).

## Concepts 32–36 (added later; UNVERIFIED: no searches possible, from memory)

### 32 · THERMAL
- Full gliding simulators: **Condor Soaring** (2002–), **Silent Wings**; paragliding mobile sims. **Glider** (John Calhoun, 1988, Mac):
  a paper glider riding air vents through a house, the classic "ride invisible rising air" game. *Flower* (thatgamecompany, 2009)
  for wind as a verb. Unchecked: art-games about soaring in thermals.

### 33 · CANUTE
- **From Dust** (Ubisoft, Éric Chahi, 2011): a god game whose whole substance is moving sand, water and lava under physics. Casual
  sandcastle builders on mobile. Unchecked: tide-versus-castle physics games.

### 34 · WOBBLE
- The wobbly-table theorem: popular via **Numberphile** (2014) and articles; Baritompa, Löwen, Polster & Ross, "Mathematical table-turning
  revisited" (2007, extends it to rectangular tables). Unchecked: interactive demos.

### 35 · TANGLE
- **Raymer & Smith, "Spontaneous knotting of an agitated string"** (PNAS 2007; Ig Nobel 2008). KnotPlot (Rob Scharein) for knot visualisation.
  Unchecked: interactive versions.

### 36 · OVERHANG
- The block-stacking / book-stacking problem (harmonic overhang) is a staple of recreational maths; **Paterson & Zwick, "Overhang"**
  (Amer. Math. Monthly 2009, Robbins Prize). Probably GeoGebra/Desmos demos exist. Unchecked.
